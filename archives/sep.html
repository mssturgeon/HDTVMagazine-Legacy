'Darkness at High Noon: The Carl Foreman Documents'

How the blacklisting of writer Carl Foreman shaped the film ``High Noon,'' for which he won an Academy Award nomination

This progra m presents a searing look at how the blacklisting of Carl Foreman and his betrayal at the hands of his colleagues defined and shaped the classic motion picture High Noon. It is August 1952. Foreman, who will receive his third Oscar nomination for his authorship of the film, is living in London, a fugitive from the blacklist. Unable to work in his homeland, and with the State Department attempting to seize his passport, he reads the outstanding review of his film in the New York Times. What should be the high point of his career is now a lonely exile; he writes a letter to the Times critic Bosley Crowther that explains and illuminates all the circumstances surrounding the making of High Noon and his colleagues' betrayal. That letter, supported by later interviews and clips of the motion picture, provides a new look at Hollywood's darkest hour. Richard Crenna narrates.
