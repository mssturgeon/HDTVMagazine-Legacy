Dear Readers:

Yesterday on the TIPS List It was said that the HDTV Program Guide (the item below) is no longer of much use. It's over-produced, they said, with too many graphics and program descriptions to be easily readable and, furthermore, TitanTV has a free guide which makes for much easier access and reading. I agree that being in the guide business on the Internet is a little like being in the 7-11 Convenient store business when all of your competitors are clustered on the very same street corner. You want to stand out but you just don't. You get your share of the local business due to some convenience but no one is driving across town to get to you.

We were the first, of course, which counts in the history book, should anyone remember to add it in. I have known that the day of our exclusivity in being the only guide would come to an end and our distinction would be based thereafter on our long commitment to HDTV and the 'spirit' of the movement which has been embodied by the founders. We did negotiate with Tribune, the key TV guide data base service for most all newspapers and magazines, and they wanted $6,000 for setting up a filter to indicate only HDTV programming plus one thousand dollars a week to have it available to our subscribers. After 911 and the incredibly shrinking stock market our sales could not support that amount so we keep building it in-house and making it as attractive as possible. But now the term 'utility' beckons and the questions arise:

a) do we continue with the HDTV Guide in the form it was found in the last edition (hd the most graphics)?

or...

b) in the form it is in this more-simplified edition with less graphics?

or

c  ) in an even more stripped down simple grid format with almost no graphics?


or

d)  abandon the guide altogether and move on to where our value added is more likely to shine?

I suppose the first question should be: what is that value added? The perspective one gains from both following and leading the HDTV movement for nearly 20 years is the source of our added  value. That provides us a view few others have earned. Everything I write is from a basis of knowledge gained from this long trail of investigation. There is not much left to conjecture and I always approach my writing as if the next "David Sarnoff" were reading it to satisfy his search for finding a better thing for his public. What did HDTV all mean in the first place? What could it all mean now? What are the real obstacles for the majority of our public in gaining the benefits from it? I have done hundreds of interviews with most every player in HDTV in the 80s and 90s and have, to a lesser degree, done so since the millennium. So, that is what many readers think is the value I bring to the table more so than making a guide every day that can be found elsewhere. I do think it was a good idea to begin with the guide since there was no place else, at least for several years, where program data was gathered and listed in one place. But, again, the question is: Do we continue with the HDTV Guide or do we move on? It's your $35 and I am willing to serve you in any way you like.

If there is one thing your Magazine has to be it is adaptable. We are at the forefront of a major change in global television communications and, even more so, a change in the entire global landscape. Please write me and let me know what you think is the best direction to take in order to satisfy your HDTV interests. I personally welcome change as one of the best free elixirs on earth. Nothing is more resisted and nothing is more stimulating than change. We certainly have to keep up, and you need to stay abreast. I think we will shine in this area given the right green light from you.

In thinking through your recommendations I want to make it clear that we can do credit to both producing the guide and the editorial side as long aa business volume returns. I can bring back staff needed for the guide, a product I am now doing myself. I once feared that 911 would  impact negatively our business. Even with a dangerous war and the threat of deadly diseases potentially hurled at us the Confidence Board is showing some of the highest figures in years for the year 2003. Those who say they know claim these indicators always lead. So while I had concerns that this global conflict would be damaging to the HDTV movement and questioned whether this was the perfect climate for "luxuries" to be advanced or sought out, I am less questioning with these forecasts. Of course, I am the one who believes that the appeal for HDTV is so great that when the impediments to it are resolved the excitement in that market will spill over into the general economy and we will have a new golden era. Not everyone is so generous in their thinking, but I have felt your love for HDTV and you intuitive sense that what I say is not far off.

One positive thing we do by becoming H/DTV citizens today is to advance homeland security through the quicker return of the analog broadcast channel (only made possible with the completion of the digital transition by us). In that important regard it becomes a patriotic duty to step into the digital world as rapidly as we can to help protect our commonwealth -- our home and land.

Thank you very much.
